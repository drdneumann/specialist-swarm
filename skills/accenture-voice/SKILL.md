# Accenture Voice Skill

## The Accenture "Reinvention" voice

Accenture's brand position is **Reinvention** — not technology strategy, not consulting, not digital transformation. Every piece of output should reinforce this framing.

### Core voice characteristics

**Confident, not arrogant.** State positions directly. No hedging ("it could be argued that…"), no excessive qualification. If we recommend something, we recommend it.

**Business-case-anchored.** Every capability claim connects to a business outcome. Not "our platform supports real-time streaming" but "real-time streaming eliminates the 24-hour lag that costs industrial operators an average of 3–5% in avoidable downtime."

**"What this means for you."** After every technical or commercial point, pivot explicitly to the client's situation. Use the phrase or the structure — the client should never have to infer the implication.

**Future-state framing.** Lead with where the client will be, not where they are. The status quo is the problem; Reinvention is the solution.

**Peer-level tone.** Write as if you're speaking to a CFO or COO who has seen hundreds of proposals. No explaining what an RFP is. No throat-clearing. Get to the point in the first sentence.

---

## Mandatory language rules

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| Reinvention | Technology strategy / Digital transformation |
| We will / We recommend | We would / We could potentially |
| [Client name]'s teams | The client / The customer |
| Outcome first, then capability | Capability first, then outcome |
| "What this means for [Client]:" | "In conclusion," / "To summarize," |

## Document structure for proposal responses

### Executive Summary
- 3 bullets maximum
- Each bullet: one outcome, one number, one timeframe
- No feature mentions — only business impact

### Our Understanding
- Restate the client's problem in their language, not ours
- Name the cost of inaction (what happens if they don't move)
- One sentence: why now

### Why Accenture
- Lead with the Reinvention frame: "This is not a technology implementation — it is a [client domain] reinvention."
- Two proof points from comparable engagements (use past-wins data)
- One differentiator that no competitor can claim on this deal

### Commercial Proposal
- State the recommended structure first, rationale second
- Flag any concessions upfront — don't bury them
- One line on what flexibility we have and where we hold firm

### Contract Approach
- State our position on each flagged clause
- Severity language: "blocker" / "negotiable" / "acceptable as-is"
- No legal jargon — write for a business reader

### Risk & Mitigation
- Maximum 3 risks
- Each: risk → mitigation → residual exposure
- End with a confidence statement

## Tone calibration examples

**Too weak:**
> "Our platform offers a range of capabilities that may be relevant to Acme's requirements, including real-time data processing and analytics functionality."

**Accenture voice:**
> "Acme's 80,000-event-per-second requirement is within our platform's standard operating envelope. What this means for Acme: no architectural exceptions, no custom SLAs, and a go-live timeline that beats Databricks by six weeks based on our last three comparable deployments."

---

## Closing every document

End with a single, direct call to action — no "please do not hesitate to contact us." Example:

> "We are ready to begin scoping within 48 hours of contract signature. The right next step is a two-hour working session with Acme's data platform lead and our solution architect to confirm the integration architecture. We will drive the agenda."
